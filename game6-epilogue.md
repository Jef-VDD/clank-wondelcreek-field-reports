### PROLOGUE

Back above ground, a message from Omin was waiting. The world was, regrettably, still scheduled for destruction by an extradimensional horror — but there was a place called the **Museum of the Lost** that kept records of fallen civilizations, and it might know who built the pyramid. Reports placed it somewhere to the south. The franchise was to find it.

The southern reaches opened up: new roads, new mountains, new places to be eaten.

---

### FIELD OPERATIONS — DETAILED ACCOUNT

### THE MENAGERIE

**Piko** headed for a circus tent where Bearnum — a dire bear in a tuxedo — presided over a menagerie of alarmingly familiar monsters. Then the ground shook with Zrog Mogolth's touch, Bearnum tore the tent open, the exhibits scattered, and Portentia Dran was already at the ringmaster's elbow. "Stay in your seat, chump. My crew will handle this."

The franchise tried. But nobody had the combat experience yet to bring down the Globulous Prism, and by the time Piko returned, Dran Enterprises had handled it — the creatures hunted down with disturbing enthusiasm, and Dran hired as the circus's new security. Portentia's influence spread, and with it Malathrex's hunger: his attacks would strike truer from now on.

### THE WESTERN RIVALRY

**Sir Prize** and **Bonkbeard** took the same road west. This was not a coincidence. After two missions of watching the dwarf collect trophies through luck, stubbornness, and the active refusal to help anyone, the warrior had decided enough was enough. Same route. Same targets. Sir Prize would simply get there first.

And Sir Prize did — every time. Through planning, through cunning, through actually paying attention to where the roads went. Bonkbeard trailed behind, finding empty pedestals and the faint smell of smug.

### THE CIRCLE

In a skittish little town, an old woman at the inn complained that their protective magic circle was failing. **Sir Prize** found it — faded, barely visible — and in a single furious burst of effort completed the ritual to restore it. The circle flared to life. The townsfolk gasped. The warrior turned to the crowd with a flourish:

"You are welcome, good citizens! Acquisitions Incorporated is always here to help! At very affordable rates!"

A few onlookers took business cards. The town went on the map. It did not get a name. Nobody had brought a decent pen.

### THE RANSOM NOTE

An arrow buried itself in a tree three feet from **Bonkbeard's** head, a ransom note tied to the shaft. A companion had been taken. The kidnappers wanted instructions followed to the letter; Bonkbeard tracked them instead — straight past the guards and into a tent where the hostage was making shadow puppets by lantern light.

"Oh, thank goodness! This has been really boring — but also terrifying." They were fairly sure they'd been scheduled for execution in the morning. The bandits had stored their loot in the same tent as their prisoner, a decision Bonkbeard declined to critique.

### THE STRANGE LIGHTS

**Piko** climbed a mountain to investigate strange swirls of light — and arrived just as Malathrex snapped his jaws shut on the figure conjuring them. The dragon read the papers the figure dropped and turned to the goblin. "Softling, a new entity is laying claim to my lands. Have you anything to tell me about someone named Zrog Mogolth?"

Piko asked the dragon for help.

Malathrex very pointedly did not laugh. "I thought you might be made of sterner stuff. It is just as well. This Zrog Mogolth will discover that its new claim is no easy prize." Piko told him everything about the pyramid and the gate — carefully leaving out who, exactly, had opened it.

### THE SMUGGLER

At the foot of a waterfall, **Sir Prize** startled an elven smuggler into drawing steel. Once it was clear the warrior wasn't there to rob him, the elf offered a deal: help with a few "deliveries," no questions asked. A new companion with a boat, who — like an increasing number of franchise assets — has yet to be given a name.

### THE WITCH'S BOOK

The old woman at the cauldron was not herself. Her eyes were pure black. Ichor traced a line down one cheek. Her voice echoed with something far too large for her frail body, and the book she held out to **Sir Prize** radiated the same noxious aura the franchise had felt at the pyramid gate. "Did you bring your book for this week? Mine's about a powerful new source of magic. Only mild side effects!"

Sir Prize could have thrown it in the cauldron. Would have, once. Instead the warrior took the book and walked away. If beating Bonkbeard meant a little darkness, so be it.

### THE FAIR

At an adventurer academy's networking fair, the organizer recognized **Harry's** Acquisitions Incorporated pin — fame, finally — and handed the wizard an intern and a cut of the loot from the fair's ruined-castle exhibit in exchange for his "professional perspective." Harry gave it freely. The interns did not ask what it was.

### THE MINER

Late in the mission, in a mountain cave, **Bonkbeard** followed the clang of a pick on stone and found a lone dwarven miner working a copper vein that had almost nothing left to give. She wiped her brow, offered him hardtack, and asked whether he might be headed anywhere with more rock in it.

"Honestly, I don't even do anything with the ore. It's not about that for me. As long as there's something to mine, I'm happy."

Bonkbeard looked at her. She looked at him. Two dwarves, one cave, a shared understanding that stone is its own reward and ore is a side effect. No further negotiation was required. She joined the franchise, and remains — in keeping with the theme of the mission — nameless.

### DUKE DESTRIN

The amulet business had hung over the franchise since the third mission, and **Bonkbeard** was done waiting. He drew his weapon in the Duke's hall, and Destrin's forced smile became a real one: "In truth, I had hoped this day would come." The Duke dissolved into mist and came at him — but the dwarf had come with a holy symbol, a garlic wreath, and a great deal of patience finally run out. Destrin's art collection did not survive. Neither did Destrin.

The amulet was in dwarven hands. Returning it to the old vampire on the ruin would have to wait for the Underworld.

### THE DEAD END

Here is where karma was supposed to arrive.

Sir Prize had claimed every artifact on the western road. Bonkbeard should have been heading home with nothing but a dead Duke to show for it, finally paying for a career built on refusing to help. Instead, the dwarf wandered onto a lonely overlook at the far end of a path that led nowhere — because, as he would insist afterward, "why would there be a path if there was nothing at the end of it?"

An old woman sat there in meditation, dressed like one of Malathrex's cultists but with none of the fanaticism. "In my day, the faith was about harmony with dragons. Now they just use it as an excuse to share bad poetry." She taught Bonkbeard a little about the real faith — enough that one of his rubies would never again draw the dragon's eye.

And beside her, waiting as if placed there for him: a very, very valuable artifact. The kind that looks excellent in an employee review. "Might not get fired after all," the dwarf observed.

Somewhere down the mountain, Sir Prize's braids tightened.

### THE DJINN

**Harry** found himself in the same spot Bonkbeard had just escaped: every worthwhile artifact already claimed, the mission running out, nothing in the pack but an intern's résumé. So the wizard climbed a staircase carved into a mountainside, up through the clouds, to a temple of silver and gold — because at that point, why not.

A golden lamp sat on the altar. Harry, being of an acquiring disposition, gave it a rub.

"You like it? It was nice as a summer place, but I outgrew it long ago!" A burly djinn loomed over him, grinning. "Visitors here are so rare! I, Baldander, welcome you!" After a very long conversation about the world below, Baldander declared that the Compact obliged him to assist. Harry walked away with a djinn on retainer and the temple's artifact in his pack — the same lucky break the dwarf had caught, arriving just late enough to change nothing.

He did not walk all the way home. The wizard surfaced, took in the sight of The Drunken Dragon in the distance, and collapsed — safely above ground, djinn and artifact intact, inn unreached.

### THE MUSEUM OF THE LOST

It was **Piko** who found it: a columned building of red marble on an island, a wooden desk, a white bell of bone that made no sound when rung. A door opened anyway. A hooded, faceless figure glided out.

"We are the Curator. We collect knowledge of peoples and worlds that no longer exist. You will be joining this group shortly, yes?"

The Curator could help with the pyramid's builders — for a price. Something of the franchise's own, so they too could be remembered when they were gone. "Return in a few days. For now... enjoy the gift shop."

Piko earned the **Associate Spotlight**. The gift shop was not reviewed.

### THE CURATOR'S PRICE

At mission's end, the franchise returned to the Museum. Payment was due. As keeper of the franchise's hoard, **Piko** chose what to give: the **Frowned Upon Tome**, surrendered to the eternities. The Curator tucked it into a sleeve and glided away. The chain on the great book fell open.

Inside: the history of the pyramid's builders. They had raised a network of **six elemental shrines** that locked something terrible out of this world, and the shrines kept the lock closed.

There was nothing in the book about closing it again once it was open.

### HOME OFFICE NOTES

*Sir Prize ran the cleanest campaign of the mission: right route, right priorities, first to every target, a magic circle restored in an afternoon. The warrior finished second, by a margin the Home Office would describe as humiliating, to a dwarf who found treasure at the end of a road that went nowhere. When asked for comment, Sir Prize referred to Bonkbeard as a "rotzakske." Our linguists could not place the dialect. They do recognize the sentiment. They have declined to translate it, citing tone. Sir Prize has also filed a transfer request — not for the warrior, but for the dwarf, to any franchise other than this one. The request has been noted. The Home Office has no further notes. The Home Office has only sympathy.*

*Bonkbeard slew Duke Destrin, which was overdue and reflects well on the franchise. The dwarf also credits his dead-end discovery to "suspecting there'd be something there." We have reviewed his suspicion. It was luck. We note for the record that Bonkbeard has, over six missions, traded away two footholds to Portentia, the goodwill of every colleague, and the occasional franchise obligation — and each time walked home heavier than when he left. No sacrifice is too great, provided someone else is making it. Several associates have asked whether the Home Office prefers jolly cooperation or cutthroat competition. The Home Office prefers results. How the franchise arrives at them is a matter of personal growth, and the Home Office would never deprive its associates of that. We will say only that the trophy has a name on it, the name is singular, and we see no reason to order a second trophy.*

*A Dragon Egg last mission. A Zrog-tainted tome this one. Sir Prize insists this is not a descent into darkness but "a proportionate response to a dwarf who has never once shared." The Home Office is not saying the warrior is turning. We are saying we have pre-filled the form, and that Bonkbeard's name appears in the "contributing factors" box three times.*

*Piko told Malathrex about the pyramid while omitting who opened it. This is either excellent diplomacy or the setup for a very bad future conversation. Possibly both.*

*Harry has now failed to reach the inn in three of the last four missions. The wizard reports no concerns. The djinn reports no concerns. The Home Office has concerns.*

*On the matter of names. This mission the franchise acquired a town, a dwarven miner, an elven smuggler, and a companion still awaiting an alignment and a personal effect — and named none of them. The research notes on Zrog Mogolth, compiled after the pyramid incident, also still bear no author, despite the rule being simply "write your own name on it." The franchise cites a shortage of adequate pens. The Home Office has enclosed several. Please use them before the next filing. Unnamed assets cannot be insured.*

*The shrines are the key, and nobody knows how to re-lock the gate. The franchise returns to the Underworld. The amulet still needs returning. The dwarf still needs beating. Priorities may vary by associate.*

*— Filed on behalf of Omin Dran, CEO*
