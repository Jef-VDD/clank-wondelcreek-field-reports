### MISSION SUMMARY

The newly formed franchise set up operations in the old roadside inn now christened **The Drunken Dragon**, located in the township of **Wondelcreek**. Their mission: retrieve an artifact from the surrounding wilds and return to HQ alive, thereby completing their application to Acquisitions Incorporated. Simple enough, on paper.

### FIELD OPERATIONS — DETAILED ACCOUNT

The team deployed from The Drunken Dragon with the quiet confidence of people who have never been chased by a dragon. This would change.

### COACH TROUBLES

The very first order of business was a logistics crisis. Deep in the frontier, the team encountered an eccentric representative of Comfort Coaches, who was altogether too enthusiastic about a station platform currently occupied by a rather territorial dragonling. The creature had made itself quite at home, devouring pieces of every building, bench, and signpost as if sampling a party platter. Through a combination of quick thinking and quicker feet, the situation was resolved and coach travel was secured for the region — a small victory that would pay dividends in future expeditions.

### A BUBBLING CAULDRON AND A QUICK NAP

**Piko**, displaying the particular brand of fearless curiosity that comes with being small enough to hide in a soup tureen, was among the first to make meaningful contact with the locals. The little goblin-or-gnome stumbled upon an old woman tending a bubbling cauldron in the woods. Never one to turn down a hot meal — or a suspicious errand — Piko happily agreed to assist with her "recipe." One hopes the ingredients were ethically sourced.

Piko's enthusiasm, however, outpaced the tiny legs carrying it. Not long after, the brave little adventurer was knocked out — safely above ground, mercifully — having given absolutely everything to the cause. A well-earned nap, by all accounts. The Drunken Dragon's smallest employee had punched well above its weight class.

### THE LODGE AND THE LAST ARTIFACT

**Bonkbeard** made his way to the sturdy lodge of the Greater Ridge Area Explorers Society, where their president took an immediate liking to the straightforward dwarf. "We have a big season ahead of us," she told him, already handing him paperwork. The Society had work that needed doing — heavy metals to recover, and reports of secret tunnels to the north that demanded investigation. Bonkbeard, who had come looking for treasure and not a second job, nevertheless agreed to help. The beard inspires trust. The hammer inspires compliance.

There was, however, a problem. While Bonkbeard had been networking and accepting contracts, his so-called teammates had been busy snatching up every accessible artifact on the map. The dwarf surveyed his options and found exactly one remaining: high in the mountains, deep in dragon territory, as far from safety as one could reasonably get.

Bonkbeard sighed, adjusted his hammer, and began to climb.

### THE MOUNTAIN AND THE NEAR MISS

What followed was a desperate affair. True to dwarven stubbornness, Bonkbeard reached the mountain artifact — but the cost was steep. Malathrex's attention was now firmly, and uncomfortably, fixed upon him. The dragon's fury rained down as the dwarf scrambled back toward civilization, his health dwindling with every attack.

The dragon's attacks intensified — each strike landing closer than the last, each burst of flame singeing the dwarf's retreating boots. Bonkbeard had almost nothing left to give. The margin between "heroic escape" and "cautionary tale" had narrowed to almost nothing.

By the skin of his magnificent beard, Bonkbeard reached the Wizard's Tower Vrizagon and managed to claw his way to the surface — only to collapse just past the safety line. Knocked out above ground, but *above ground*, which is the important distinction between "returning with something to show for it" and "returning with nothing at all." The beard survived. The dignity, less so.

### THE CANOE GAMBIT

It was Harry who made the defining strategic play of the mission. While his companions were busy wrestling with cultists and navigating collapsed infrastructure, the wizard quietly invested in a canoe. A *canoe*. While others eyed powerful weapons and mystical tomes, Harry purchased a small watercraft and said nothing.

This would prove to be either a masterwork of tactical brilliance or an extraordinary stroke of luck. Harry maintains it was the former. The records are inconclusive.

With waterways now open to him, Harry navigated routes his competitors could not follow, claiming his artifact with an efficiency that bordered on suspicious. But the true genius — or lunacy — of the canoe investment would only reveal itself at the end of the mission.

### THE BRIDGE THAT WASN'T THERE

As the associates began their return to The Drunken Dragon, **Sir Prize** arrived at the river crossing to find a scene of devastation. The bridge was gone — replaced by a heap of rotten timbers and a distinctly unpleasant residue. The warrior surveyed the wreckage with the expression of someone who had been counting on that bridge very much.

But Sir Prize did not earn that questionable title by giving up. With tireless legs and sheer determination, the warrior took the long way around — the *very* long way around — and through a grueling forced march, became the first associate to return to The Drunken Dragon with artifact in hand. **Sir Prize claimed the Associate Spotlight**, proving that sometimes the scenic route is the winning route.

And then there was Harry. The wizard, paddling serenely in his canoe, simply went around the collapsed bridge entirely. The waterways don't care about missing bridges. Harry glided into HQ with the calm demeanor of someone who had planned for this exact scenario.

Had he, though? Had Harry — a wizard who bought a canoe before anyone else thought to — somehow *known* the bridge would fall? Or was it simply the most spectacular stroke of luck in franchise history?

Harry maintains it was foresight. The records are inconclusive. The canoe offers no comment.

### THE SECRET HARRY KEPT

The final insult — or, as Harry would call it, "strategic information management" — came at the expense of Sir Prize. The warrior had fought valiantly, marched the long way home, and scored admirably. One more hired sword or a single piece of well-timed equipment would have been enough to overtake Harry for the win.

Harry knew this. Harry said nothing.

When pressed later, the wizard simply smiled and noted that Acquisitions Incorporated rewards *individual* initiative. Sir Prize's braids quivered with indignation, but the scoreboard does not lie. Harry took first place. Sir Prize settled for a very respectable — and very bitter — second.
