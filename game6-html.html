Prologue

Back above ground, a message from Omin was waiting. The world was, regrettably, still scheduled for destruction by an extradimensional horror — but there was a place called the Museum of the Lost that kept records of fallen civilizations, and it might know who built the pyramid. Reports placed it somewhere to the south. The franchise was to find it.

The southern reaches opened up: new roads, new mountains, new places to be eaten.

Field Operations — Detailed Account
The Menagerie

Piko found a circus. Bearnum — a dire bear in a tuxedo — presided over a menagerie of monsters that looked uncomfortably like things the franchise had fought before. Then the ground shook with Zrog Mogolth's touch, Bearnum tore the tent open, the exhibits scattered, and Portentia Dran was already at the ringmaster's elbow. "Stay in your seat, chump. My crew will handle this."

For once, the franchise actually worked together. Harry and Bonkbeard took the small creatures — the ones you can handle without much experience. Sir Prize took the middleweight. That left Piko with the Globulous Prism, the biggest of the lot, who found out the hard way that a goblin alone wasn't enough. By the time anyone could help, Dran Enterprises had finished the job — with disturbing enthusiasm — and been hired as the circus's new security.

Portentia's influence spread. Malathrex's attacks would strike truer from now on. The franchise had cooperated and still lost. Bonkbeard noted that cooperation left him empty handed.

The Western Rivalry

Sir Prize and Bonkbeard took the same road west. This was not a coincidence. After two missions of watching the dwarf collect trophies through luck, stubbornness, and the active refusal to help anyone, the warrior had decided enough was enough. Same route. Same targets. Sir Prize would simply get there first.

And Sir Prize did — every time. Bonkbeard trailed behind, finding empty pedestals and the faint smell of smug. For once, the dwarf was the one eating dust.

The Circle

In a skittish little town, an old woman at the inn complained that their protective magic circle was failing. Sir Prize found it — faded, barely visible — and in a single furious burst of effort completed the ritual to restore it. The townsfolk gasped. The warrior turned to the crowd with a flourish:

"You are welcome, good citizens! Acquisitions Incorporated is always here to help! At very affordable rates!"

A few onlookers took business cards. The town went on the map and did not get a name. Nobody had brought a decent pen.

The Ransom Note

An arrow buried itself in a tree three feet from Bonkbeard's head, a ransom note tied to the shaft. A companion had been taken. The kidnappers wanted instructions followed to the letter; Bonkbeard tracked them instead — straight past the guards and into a tent where the hostage was making shadow puppets by lantern light.

"Oh, thank goodness! This has been really boring — but also terrifying." The bandits had stored their loot in the same tent as their prisoner. Bonkbeard took both. Rescue is rescue.

The Strange Lights

Piko climbed a mountain to investigate strange swirls of light and arrived just as Malathrex snapped his jaws shut on the figure conjuring them. The dragon read the papers the figure dropped and turned to the goblin. "Softling, a new entity is laying claim to my lands. Have you anything to tell me about someone named Zrog Mogolth?"

Piko could have denied everything. Could have bluffed. Instead the goblin looked up at the only creature in the region bigger than the problem and asked for help. Not a tactic — Piko simply reasoned that something this size needed a higher power on the case, and the franchise was fresh out of those.

Malathrex very pointedly did not laugh. "I thought you might be made of sterner stuff. It is just as well. This Zrog Mogolth will discover that its new claim is no easy prize." Piko told him everything about the pyramid and the gate — carefully leaving out who had opened it. Piko has always been good at leaving things out.

The Smuggler

At the foot of a waterfall, Sir Prize startled an elven smuggler into drawing steel. Once it was clear the warrior wasn't there to rob him, the elf offered a deal: a few "deliveries," no questions asked. A new companion, a boat, and no name to write down.

The Witch's Book

The old woman at the cauldron was not herself. Her eyes were pure black. Ichor traced a line down one cheek. The book she held out to Sir Prize carried the same noxious aura as the pyramid gate. "Did you bring your book for this week? Mine's about a powerful new source of magic. Only mild side effects!"

Nobody at the table tried to stop him. Nobody needed to. The warrior had spent five missions being the one who does the right thing and watching it earn nothing; a little darkness felt like a fair trade for finally beating the dwarf. Sir Prize took the book.

Bonkbeard said nothing. Who was he to tell anyone how to play? If anything, the dwarf looked quietly pleased — after five missions of colleagues asking him to cooperate, he finally found a competitor.

The Fair

At an adventurer academy's networking fair, the organizer recognized Harry's Acquisitions Incorporated pin and handed the wizard an intern and a cut of the loot from the fair's ruined-castle exhibit in exchange for his "professional perspective." It was the high point of Harry's mission. The high point of Harry's mission... was a networking event.

The Miner

Late in the mission, in a mountain cave, Bonkbeard followed the clang of a pick on stone and found a lone dwarven miner working a copper vein with almost nothing left in it. She offered him hardtack and asked whether he might be headed anywhere with more rock. "I don't even do anything with the ore. It's not about that for me." Two dwarves, one cave, a shared understanding that stone is its own reward. No further negotiation was required.

She joined the franchise. Nobody wrote her name down. Where are those damn pens!

Duke Destrin

This was not planned. Bonkbeard had not set out to kill a vampire. He simply found himself at the end of the mission standing in Duke Destrin's hall with a holy symbol, a garlic wreath, and a grudge the franchise had been carrying since the third mission. The tools were there. The Duke was there. The dwarf drew his weapon.

Destrin's forced smile became a real one. "In truth, I had hoped this day would come." He dissolved into mist and came at him. Destrin's art collection did not survive. Neither did Destrin.

Then the amulet. Keep it, or return it to the old vampire on the ruin? Bonkbeard chose to return it — partly because it made the better story, and partly because the last time anyone trusted a Destrin with a valuable item, Sir Prize ended up losing a valuable artifact for it. The dwarf had learned that lesson secondhand. The return would have to wait for the Underworld.

The Dead End

Here is where karma was supposed to arrive.

Sir Prize had claimed every artifact on the western road. Bonkbeard should have been heading home with nothing but a dead Duke, finally paying for a career built on refusing to help. Instead the dwarf wandered onto a lonely overlook at the far end of a path that went nowhere — because, as he would insist afterward, "why would there be a path if there was nothing at the end of it?"

An old woman sat there in meditation, dressed like one of Malathrex's cultists but with none of the fanaticism. "In my day, the faith was about harmony with dragons. Now they just use it as an excuse to share bad poetry." She taught Bonkbeard a little about the real faith — enough that one of his rubies would never again draw the dragon's eye.

And beside her, waiting as if placed there for him: a very, very valuable artifact. The kind that looks excellent in an employee review. "Might not get fired after all," the dwarf observed.

Somewhere down the mountain, Sir Prize heard the news and said nothing for a long moment. Then, quietly: "Again?" Then, less quietly: that no one is this lucky, that the dwarf has a map the rest of them don't — or that the thing behind the pyramid gate made him an offer on the way down, and the "resistance" was for show. Bonkbeard offered no defense, possibly because the artifact was heavy and he had no hands free.

The Djinn

Harry was having the mission Bonkbeard should have had. Every worthwhile artifact gone, time running out, nothing in the pack but an intern's résumé. This was not the canoe wizard of the first mission. This was a wizard climbing a staircase into the clouds because it was the only direction left.

At the top, a temple. On the altar, a golden lamp. Harry rubbed it. "Visitors here are so rare! I, Baldander, welcome you!" A burly djinn, a very long conversation about the world below, and an obligation under something called the Compact. Harry walked away with a djinn on retainer and the temple's artifact in his pack — the same lucky break the dwarf had caught, arriving just late enough to change nothing.

He did not walk all the way home. The wizard surfaced, saw The Drunken Dragon in the distance, and collapsed — safely above ground, djinn and artifact intact, inn unreached.

The Museum of the Lost

It was Piko who found it: a columned building of red marble on an island, a wooden desk, a white bell of bone that made no sound when rung. A door opened anyway. A hooded, faceless figure glided out.

"We are the Curator. We collect knowledge of peoples and worlds that no longer exist. You will be joining this group shortly, yes?"

The Curator could help with the pyramid's builders — for a price. Something of the franchise's own. "Return in a few days. For now... enjoy the gift shop."

Piko earned the Associate Spotlight. The gift shop was not reviewed.

The Curator's Price

At mission's end the franchise returned. Payment was due, and it fell to Piko to choose what the franchise could live without. There was a brief debate. There was not a long one. The Frowned Upon Tome went into the Curator's sleeve, and nobody frowned.

The chain on the great book fell open. Inside: the history of the pyramid's builders. They had raised a network of six elemental shrines that locked something terrible out of this world, and the shrines kept the lock closed.

There was nothing in the book about closing it again once it was open.

Sir Prize ran the cleanest campaign of the mission: right route, right priorities, first to every target, a magic circle restored in an afternoon. The warrior finished second anyway. When asked for comment, Sir Prize referred to Bonkbeard as a "rotzakske." Our linguists could not place the dialect. They do recognize the sentiment, citing tone. We also note a Dragon Egg last mission and a Zrog-tainted tome this one. The Home Office is not saying the warrior is turning. We are saying we have pre-filled the form, and that Bonkbeard's name appears in the "contributing factors" box three times. Sir Prize has additionally filed a transfer request — not for the warrior, but for the dwarf, to any franchise other than this one — and a formal theory that the dwarf's fortunes improved after his conversation with the voice in the pyramid and, less coherently, something about secret books. The Home Office finds no evidence for this. The Home Office has also not looked.

Bonkbeard slew Duke Destrin and credits his dead-end discovery to "suspecting there'd be something there." We have reviewed his suspicion. It was luck. The dwarf denies any arrangement with extradimensional entities; he was not asked about dimensional ones. We note for the record that Bonkbeard has, over six missions, traded away two footholds to Portentia, the goodwill of every colleague, and the occasional franchise obligation — and each time walked home heavier than when he left. No sacrifice is too great, provided someone else is making it. Several associates have asked whether the Home Office prefers jolly cooperation or cutthroat competition. The Home Office prefers results. How the franchise arrives at them is a matter of personal growth, and the Home Office would never deprive its associates of that. Home Office recognizes one Most Valuable Employee per mission. The Second Most Valuable Employee is welcome to recognize themselves.

Piko asked Malathrex for help with Zrog Mogolth and told the dragon about the pyramid while omitting who opened it. The Home Office notes that this is the only associate who appears to think the world ending is a problem worth solving rather than a scoring opportunity. We are monitoring this attitude. It may be contagious. It may also be the only reason anyone is still alive.

Harry has now failed to reach the inn in three of the last four missions. The wizard reports no concerns. The djinn reports no concerns. The Home Office has concerns.

On the matter of names. This mission the franchise acquired a town, a dwarven miner, an elven smuggler, and a companion still awaiting an alignment and a personal effect — and wrote down none of them. The franchise cites a shortage of adequate pens. The Home Office has enclosed several. Please use them before the next filing. Unnamed assets cannot be insured.

The shrines are the key, and nobody knows how to re-lock the gate. The franchise returns to the Underworld. The amulet still needs returning. The dwarf still needs beating. Priorities may vary by associate.
