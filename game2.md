### PROLOGUE

This morning, a scroll case appeared on the common room table of The Drunken Dragon. Inside: an impossible number of pages listing company duties, regulations, and milestones, topped with a letter from Omin Dran himself, the CEO of Acquisitions Incorporated.

The gist: your application has been fast-tracked. Welcome to the team. Don't worry about the fine print. Not worth reading at all, really.

Each associate signed the Charter and received their role.

| Associate | Role |
|---|---|
| **Harry** | Obviator |
| **Sir Prize** | Cartographer |
| **Bonkbeard** | Decisionist |
| **Piko** | Hoardsperson |

On the back of Omin's letter, a postscript warned of a certain **Portentia Dran** — Omin's evil sibling, already expanding her own rival company, **Dran Enterprises**, into the region. The Drunken Dragon franchise was instructed to make sure Acquisitions Incorporated gets a piece of whatever treasure Portentia is after.
But first, there was the matter of the bridge — or rather, the lack of one. The collapse that had caused Sir Prize so much grief last mission had not been repaired, so the franchise's first order of business was to bushwhack a new trail south from The Drunken Dragon through the woodlands, bypassing the ruined crossing entirely.  The wilds were now open for business.

### FIELD OPERATIONS — DETAILED ACCOUNT

### SIR PRIZE'S PERFECT HAND

Before anyone had even left The Drunken Dragon's front door, fortune had already chosen its favorite. **Sir Prize** uncovered two major secrets in rapid succession — each one stamped with the Acquisitions Incorporated Seal of Excellence. Two Seals. Combined with a third earned from a companion the warrior had personally recruited — one **Barry Batsbal** — Sir Prize was now carrying three Seals of Excellence before most associates had finished lacing their boots.

This was, by any reasonable measure, a winning position. All Sir Prize had to do was retrieve an artifact and walk home.

### PIKO MEETS THE LOCALS (FIRST!)

The question of who would make first contact with the neighboring communities became a matter of heated debate between Piko and Sir Prize. Both were eager. Both were ready. But it was **Piko** — small, fast, and entirely unwilling to wait for a warrior to finish adjusting armor straps — who darted ahead and reached the locals first.

The diminutive adventurer met Duke Destrin, an effusive nobleman with a particular interest in an ancestral sword held by the dwarves in the nearby mountains. Destrin wanted it brought to him, and Piko was happy to accept the contract. The Duke's wizard advisor Vriz also had a request — something about mystic fruit and a tower to the west. Piko nodded along. Contracts were piling up. Business was good.

But the real prize from Piko's expedition was the artifact the contract revealed — a blade that now sat within arm's reach of Sir Prize's position. The very warrior who already had three Seals of Excellence now had an artifact practically delivered to the doorstep. Fate, it seemed, was being indecently generous.

### THE FATAL ENCOURAGEMENT

All Sir Prize had to do was pick up the sword, walk home, and win. The braids practically swayed with triumph.

And then the others spoke up.

"You know," they said, "since you're *right there* — why not deliver the sword to the Duke? Think of the franchise! Think of the contract! We'll handle the rest."

They were very supportive. *Very* supportive. Suspiciously supportive, one might say, given that delivering the sword meant Sir Prize would need to hand over the artifact currently guaranteeing victory and find a new one somewhere else.

Sir Prize, noble to a fault, agreed. It was the right thing to do. The team-player thing. The franchise-first thing.

The warrior marched the blade to Duke Destrin's castle. The Duke examined the runes with sparkling eyes, muttering about legendary grudge-keeping dwarves and the legal ownership of mines. "I am, of course, willing to share with my friends," he said, then paused meaningfully — "...so long as they remain loyal."

The sword was delivered. The contract was complete. The artifact was gone.

And where was the only one remaining? High in the mountains. The same mountains that had nearly killed Bonkbeard last mission — the same peaks that had left the dwarf collapsed at the gates of Vrizagon with barely a breath left in his body. The mountains had tasted franchise blood before, and they were hungry for more.

Sir Prize began the climb. The colleagues who had encouraged this decision watched from below with expressions of deep concern that may or may not have been genuine.

### ALL KINDS OF STRANGE MISFORTUNES

Meanwhile, the townsfolk of Wondelcreek had been suffering from bizarre calamities. **Harry** pitched in — reconstructing a vanished well, retrieving cows from treetops. Mayor Schroeder treated the wizard to a luncheon and an alarming quantity of pie, declaring The Drunken Dragon "a cut above those jokers from Dran Enterprises."

For his tireless service to the community, Harry earned this mission's **Associate Spotlight**.

### BONKBEARD'S LONG ROAD

**Bonkbeard** was having a wretched time of it. Every path seemed to lead nowhere, every promising trail fizzled into nothing. The dwarf trudged onward with the grim determination of someone who has been personally insulted by the universe.

And then — all at once — the luck turned. Strength surged through his limbs, and what followed was the most extraordinary burst of adventuring in Drunken Dragon franchise history.

First, an abandoned manor with "TURN BACK" and "STAY AWAY" scrawled on the walls. Bonkbeard went inside anyway, found a library full of strange journals, and was chased out by a spectral figure bursting from a cabinet. He snatched whatever papers he could and ran.

Next, a major secret — and with it, the attention of Malathrex himself. Bonkbeard, running on adrenaline, chose flattery. The dragon listened. Then yawned. "You tell me only what I already know," Malathrex rumbled, before rising lazily into the sky with a thrashing goblin dangling from one talon. Being found *boring* by a dragon was not a compliment — but it beat the alternative.

Then the wizard Vriz's cabin in the mushroom forest, where a mystic fruit delivery earned a cheerful teleport onward.

All of it — the manor, the dragon, the delivery — in a single relentless push. Earlier in the expedition, Bonkbeard and Harry had squabbled over strategy, and in the heat of it Harry had abandoned his signature canoe approach in favor of a crown for quick points. He would come to regret this. Because Bonkbeard had been paying attention, and the canoe was now uncontested.

The dwarf said nothing. He just sailed home. **First to return to The Drunken Dragon.**

The dwarf who couldn't find his footing all day. The dwarf who had been deemed *boring* by a dragon. Bonkbeard claimed his Mug, set it on the bar, and finally allowed himself a smile beneath that magnificent beard.

### THE FALL OF SIR PRIZE

Sir Prize was in the mountains now. The warrior who had started the expedition with everything — three Seals of Excellence, an artifact within arm's reach, a position that would have won the whole thing outright — was now scrambling up frozen peaks for the last remaining artifact.

The dragon was watching. The rage was building. And somewhere in the back of everyone's mind was the knowledge that **Piko had forgotten to mention something important one step too late** — word of new Pickaxes available at the Market, tools practically designed for mountain traversal, that could have made all the difference.

Was it malicious? No. Piko had simply been distracted — the life of a small adventurer is full of distractions. But the timing was exquisite in its cruelty. Sir Prize needed those pickaxes. Sir Prize did not have those pickaxes. And it was close — *so agonizingly close*.

But the mountains do not care about how close you were.

**Sir Prize went down.** Knocked out in the frozen peaks, deep in the Danger Zone, with no artifact to show for it. An empty pack and an empty report.

Not dead — not technically — but dragged back to civilization empty-handed with nothing to show for the entire mission. When Bonkbeard had collapsed in Mission 1, it was within sight of the Wizard's Tower, safely above the danger line. This was not that. This was the mountain swallowing everything Sir Prize had earned and returning nothing.

The warrior who had started the day with a winning hand. Who had been talked into delivering the sword by teammates with suspiciously bright smiles. Who had climbed into the mountains because there was no other choice left. Who would have won — *easily* won — if only the blade had stayed in the scabbard, or if a certain goblin had remembered one small detail a single step earlier.

Three Seals of Excellence. The Associate Spotlight earned through sheer grit in Mission 1. A sword delivered with honor. All of it lost to the peaks.

The Dran Enterprises track received its first X. Somewhere, Portentia Dran smiled.

### THE TUNNELS THAT WEREN'T

There was one more piece of unfinished business. The secret tunnels to the north — the ones Bonkbeard had been contracted to investigate back in Mission 1 — remained unexplored. Nobody had made the trek. Nobody had checked.

This did not go unnoticed by certain parties. Another X on the Dran Enterprises track. The competition was gaining ground, and the franchise hadn't even realized it.

The shadows of Dran Enterprises were growing longer.

### UNFINISHED BUSINESS

On the way back, **Piko** passed by the old woman's cauldron once more. She looked up expectantly, stirring with one hand and holding out the other. Piko had nothing to give. The woman's expression didn't change, but her stirring slowed — just slightly — in a way that suggested her patience would not last forever.
